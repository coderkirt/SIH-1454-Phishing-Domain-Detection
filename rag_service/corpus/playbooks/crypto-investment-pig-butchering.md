## How the scam works

The attacker initiates contact via messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram) or dating platforms, often pretending
to have reached the wrong number. Over weeks, the attacker builds rapport and friendship before introducing
their lucrative "side business" — trading cryptocurrency, decentralized liquidity mining, or AI trading bots.

The victim is directed to deposit USDT, Bitcoin, or Ethereum into a fraudulent trading platform or customized
mobile application. The fake platform displays simulated profits and may allow a small initial withdrawal to build
trust. When the victim attempts to withdraw larger sums, the platform freezes assets, demanding additional "taxes",
"gas fees", or "VIP verification deposits" until the victim is completely drained.

## Common message patterns

- "Sorry, I thought this was Linda's number. By the way, you seem very friendly. Are you into investing?"
- "My uncle is a senior market analyst who predicts market trends with 100% accuracy. I made 30% today."
- "Your account is temporarily locked for anti-money laundering review. Deposit 25% tax in USDT to release funds."

These schemes exploit personal trust, greed, and the irreversible nature of cryptocurrency transactions.

## Warning signs for analysts

- Trading platforms operating on newly registered domains with unverified contact information or generic templates.
- Requests to connect Web3 wallets to obscure dApp smart contracts that request unlimited token approvals.
- High, guaranteed returns (e.g. 5–15% daily or weekly) with zero downside risk.
- Demands for upfront fee deposits before accumulated account balances can be withdrawn.

## Recommended user guidance

Never send cryptocurrency or connect decentralized wallets to platforms recommended by online acquaintances.
Legitimate exchanges deduct trading and transaction fees directly from account balances rather than requiring
separate upfront deposits. Treat any investment platform promising guaranteed double-digit returns as fraudulent.
